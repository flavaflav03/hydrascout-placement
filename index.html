Hydrascout Placement

Find the most representative spots in a field to install Hydrascout soil & temperature sensors.

1 Your irrigation blocks

A block is a part of the farm watered on its own (one valve or one sector). HSTI recommends one probe per block. Not sure? Leave it empty and the app suggests a number from the field size.

Your blocks

    Name each block, then press Draw borders and tap its corners on the map. Find the farm in step 2 first if the map is not on it. Each block gets one probe, away from its borders; a block with clearly different parts gets an optional second one. Drawn blocks also set the field, so step 3 becomes optional.

    2 Find the field

      Puts the pin on the device's last GPS position, read through HSTI Logger Overview (log in there if asked).

      3 Outline the field

      No field yet.

      Tap the map to place corners, then Finish. You can also import a GeoJSON or KML boundary (e.g. from your farm software or Google Earth). Optional when the blocks are drawn in step 1: the outline around them is used.

      SoilGrids 250 m maps: they show regional patterns, so expect only a few cells over one field. The key is on the map; tap the map to see the soil at a spot. For the detailed French soil map, open the carte des sols on cartes.gouv.fr and click the field.

      4 Crop vigour (NDVI)

      The spots use the date shown.

      Recommended. The app searches free Sentinel-2 images (10 m) of the last 14 months, skips cloudy ones and picks the clearest image with full crop cover. Use the timeline to see how the field changes through the season. You can also upload your own NDVI map from a drone.

      7 Recommended spots

      Drag a numbered pin on the map to adjust a spot (an obstacle, a drain, an access path). Its notes, the report and the exports follow the new position.

        Return on investment

        A Hydrascout probe typically saves 10–30% of irrigation water, and the pumping hours, electricity or diesel that go with it. Ask the farmer for their own figures: the closer they are, the more convincing the result.
